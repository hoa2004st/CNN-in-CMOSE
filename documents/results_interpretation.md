# Results Interpretation Notes

Per-artifact takeaways for Chapter 4. Each entry: the figure/table, its one-sentence
verdict, the supporting numbers, and the sentence(s) to write. All artifacts are generated by
`python -m src.analysis.make_thesis_artifacts` into `outputs/thesis/{figures,tables}`.

Primary metrics throughout: **QWK** (quadratic-weighted kappa — penalizes far-apart ordinal
errors) and **macro-accuracy** (mean per-class recall — exposes minority-class collapse).
Raw accuracy is reported but treated as secondary because the datasets are heavily imbalanced.

---

## 1. Datasets — `dataset_class_distribution_*` / Table T1

**Verdict:** all three datasets are severely imbalanced toward the middle-upper engagement
classes, and CMOSE and DaiSEE are imbalanced in *different* directions — which foreshadows the
cross-domain gap.

- CMOSE (12,197 clips): 69.4% Engage, only 2.8% Highly-Disengage.
- DaiSEE (8,571 clips): 50.3% Engage **and 43.8% Highly-Engage**, 0.7% Highly-Disengage.
- Combined (20,768 clips): 61.5% Engage, 23.7% Highly-Engage.

**Write:** Engagement labels are ordinal and long-tailed; the rare disengaged classes (HD, DE)
are the hard, decision-relevant ones. CMOSE skews to "Engage", DaiSEE adds a large
"Highly-Engage" mass — the label priors themselves differ across corpora, motivating both
imbalance-aware losses and a cross-dataset study.

## 2. Base models × losses, in-domain — `base_models_indomain` / Table T2

**Verdict:** among monolithic baselines, an OpenFace TCN is the strongest ordinal model;
choice of loss trades raw accuracy against minority-class recall.

- Best base QWK: **openface_tcn / CE = 0.537** (accuracy 0.761).
- Highest accuracy: i3d_mlp / CE = 0.764, but its QWK is only 0.493 and macro-acc 0.508.
- Best macro-accuracy: i3d_mlp / Ordinal = 0.613 (accuracy drops to 0.686).

**Write:** TCN's temporal inductive bias edges out LSTM/Transformer/MLP on ordinal agreement;
I3D motion features give the highest raw accuracy but, under CE, do so by over-predicting the
majority class (low macro-accuracy). This sets the baseline the hybrid must beat: QWK 0.537.

## 3. Loss tradeoff — `loss_accuracy_tradeoff`

**Verdict:** cross-entropy buys accuracy by sacrificing minority classes; weighted-CE and
ordinal move up-and-left (more balanced, less accurate).

- Per model, CE points sit lower-right (high accuracy, low macro-acc); weighted-CE/ordinal
  points sit upper-left. Example (i3d_mlp): CE (acc 0.764, macro 0.508) → Ordinal (0.686, 0.613).

**Write:** There is no single best loss — it is a Pareto choice. For an engagement-monitoring
system where catching disengagement matters, the balanced losses are preferable despite lower
headline accuracy.

## 4. Training dynamics — `loss_curves_cmose_ce`

**Verdict:** all models train stably and early-stop well before the epoch cap; no overfitting
pathology — differences are architectural, not optimization artifacts.

**Write:** Validation loss is the selection metric (patience 10); dashed line marks the
restored best epoch. Curves justify the protocol and rule out under/over-training as a
confound for the comparisons.

## 5. Hybrid ablation distribution — `hybrid_ablation_distribution` (CENTERPIECE)

**Verdict:** adding the I3D stream lifts the *entire* distribution of hybrid configs above the
best baseline; the OpenFace-only hybrid is competitive but only sometimes beats it.

- Hybrid + I3D: all 32 configs cluster around median ≈ 0.547, the whole box above the
  base line (QWK 0.537); best 0.574.
- Hybrid (OpenFace only): median ≈ 0.518, straddles the base line; best 0.561.

**Write:** The semantic-group decomposition combined with I3D motion is robustly better than
the best monolithic baseline regardless of the exact per-group encoder assignment — evidence
that the *gain comes from the architecture family*, not from cherry-picking one config.

## 6. Per-group marginal effect — `hybrid_group_marginal` / Table T5 (CENTERPIECE, answers RQ2)

**Verdict:** the model is largely insensitive to the encoder choice for most groups; the one
real signal is that **head-pose wants a TCN**.

- Head pose: mean QWK TCN 0.544 vs Transformer 0.519 (**Δ = +0.025**).
- All other groups: |Δ| ≤ 0.003 (gaze +0.003, face +0.002, eye/au ≈ 0).

**Write:** Head dynamics are a temporally-local, convolution-friendly signal (nodding,
turning away) → TCN helps. Gaze/landmark/AU streams are encoder-agnostic. This is a concrete,
interpretable design rule rather than a black-box win, and honestly bounds the contribution:
most of the ablation grid is flat.

## 7. Best hybrid vs base — `hybrid_best_comparison` / Table T4

**Verdict:** the best hybrid+I3D (all-TCN) is the overall best in-domain model, but the margin
over the best baseline is incremental.

- Best hybrid+I3D `TCN_TCN_TCN_TCN_TCN`: QWK 0.574, acc 0.776, macro-acc 0.541.
- Best base (openface_tcn/CE): QWK 0.537, acc 0.761. → **+0.037 QWK, +1.5 pts accuracy.**

**Write:** State the gain plainly as incremental. The contribution is the *framework +
systematic ablation*, not a SOTA leap.

## 8. Cross-domain generalization — `crossdomain_base` / `crossdomain_hybrid` / Table T3

**Verdict:** models do not transfer across datasets; accuracy hides this, QWK exposes it; and
training on the combined corpus is the only thing that helps the unseen private set.

- Diagonal (in-domain) QWK strong (CMOSE 0.54); off-diagonal collapses: CMOSE→DaiSEE 0.02,
  DaiSEE→CMOSE 0.05 — i.e. **near chance-level ordinal agreement**.
- Off-diagonal *accuracy* looks deceptively fine (CMOSE→DaiSEE 0.49, DaiSEE→CMOSE 0.69) purely
  because predicting the majority class scores well on imbalanced targets.
- Combined → Private QWK 0.285 is the best private-set result (vs CMOSE→Private 0.19,
  DaiSEE→Private 0.11).

**Write:** This is the second headline finding. Engagement "looks" different across corpora
(different label priors + recording conditions); a model is not portable off-the-shelf.
Pooling source datasets is a simple, effective mitigation for a genuinely unseen target.
Always report QWK/kappa, not accuracy, on imbalanced engagement data.

## 8b. Why CMOSE for in-domain — `indomain_cmose_vs_daisee` / Table T7

**Verdict:** the architecture ablation is anchored on CMOSE because DAiSEE's in-domain signal is
too weak to compare models meaningfully.

- Best in-domain QWK: CMOSE 0.537 vs **DAiSEE 0.139**; macro-acc 0.535 vs 0.273.

**Write:** Use as the justification paragraph in Section 4.3. On DAiSEE the best models barely
beat chance ordinal agreement, so architecture differences there reflect label noise, not model
quality. Develop on CMOSE; validate on DAiSEE and the private set.

## 8c. Private set — `private_by_source` / `private_confusion_combined` / Table T6 (PRACTICAL HEADLINE)

**Verdict:** on the self-collected, test-only private set the architecture and combined-training
contributions compound, and the hybrid's payoff is *larger* here than in-domain.

- Best base QWK on private by source: Combined 0.285 > CMOSE 0.190 > DAiSEE 0.110.
- Best hybrid QWK on private by source: Combined **0.365** > DAiSEE 0.256 > CMOSE 0.236.
- Hybrid vs base under combined training: **0.365 vs 0.285 = +0.080** (vs +0.037 in-domain CMOSE).
- Overall best private model: combined-trained I3D hybrid `T_T_T_T_TCN` (QWK 0.365, acc 0.596).
- Private prior: 58.2% EG, 30.6% HE, 8.5% DE, 2.7% HD (test-only, 366 clips).

**Write:** This is the practical headline (Section 4.6). The private set has no in-domain option,
so it isolates "which training source generalizes best" — Combined — and shows the hybrid helps
most exactly where it matters. Both contributions stack on the hardest, most realistic target.

## 9. Error analysis — `confusion_best_models` / `per_class_f1`

**Verdict:** the hybrid's gain is concentrated in the ordinal extremes (better Highly-Engage),
but the rarest class (Highly-Disengage) remains unsolved and is even traded away.

- Best hybrid vs best base recall: Highly-Engage 0.38 → **0.50**, Disengage 0.45 → 0.51,
  Engage stable at 0.91.
- Highly-Disengage: base 0.40 → hybrid 0.26 — the hybrid sacrifices the rarest class.

**Write:** Improvement is real but uneven; HD (≈2–3% of data) is the open problem. Frame as
honest limitation and motivation for future imbalance-specific work.

---

## Headline summary for the abstract / conclusion
1. **A semantic-group hybrid (per-group temporal encoders + I3D) is the best in-domain
   engagement model** — QWK 0.574 vs 0.537 baseline — robustly across the whole ablation;
   head-pose specifically benefits from a TCN.
2. **Engagement classifiers do not generalize across datasets** (QWK collapses to ≈0
   off-diagonal); raw accuracy masks this — always report QWK/kappa on imbalanced engagement.
3. **On the self-collected private set (the real-world test), the two contributions compound:**
   combined-corpus training + the hybrid give the best result (QWK 0.365 vs 0.285 baseline,
   +0.080 — wider than in-domain), and combined training beats any single source. This is the
   practical payoff and showcases the value of the hand-labeled private set.
4. (Scope) The architecture ablation is anchored on CMOSE because DAiSEE's in-domain signal is
   too weak (QWK 0.139) to distinguish architectures from noise.
