\begin{center}
    \Large{\textbf{ABSTRACT}}\\
\end{center}
\vspace{1cm}

% (i) problem statement (why it matters, is it solved, prior approaches and their limits)
Online and hybrid education has turned the webcam into the main link between student and teacher, yet a teacher who faces many video tiles at once can no longer tell when a learner has stopped paying attention. Automatic engagement recognition addresses this by assigning a short webcam clip an ordinal level from disengaged to engaged. Prior works either encode each frame with a facial-behaviour toolkit or learn directly from raw pixels, and they report accuracy on a single dataset. Each line of work carries its own limitation. The former flattens the heterogeneous facial measurements and passes them through a single encoder, while the latter learns appearance specific to one dataset and transfers poorly to new recording conditions. A further common practice is to report accuracy, which on such imbalanced data can look healthy while the model does no better than a random guess. To address these limitations, this thesis preserves the structure of the facial signal instead of flattening it, and replaces single-dataset accuracy with an evaluation built for imbalanced ordinal labels and for transfer.
% (ii) chosen approach and (iii) overview of the solution
The proposed hybrid model splits the OpenFace descriptor into behavioural feature groups, encodes each group with its own temporal encoder, and optionally adds a global I3D motion stream, training every stream with a small auxiliary head. Every model is then selected and compared on an ordinal agreement measure that majority prediction cannot inflate, and its generalisation is measured directly across datasets.
% (iv) main contributions and final results
An ablation over the full space of encoder assignments shows that the hybrid family outperforms the strongest single-encoder baseline. A test-only private set of webcam clips recorded in the wild was collected and hand-labelled, and a cross-dataset study shows that engagement models transfer poorly and that accuracy conceals this collapse while the ordinal measure exposes it. Trained on the combined datasets, the proposed hybrid model generalises best to the unseen private set, and its margin over the baseline widens out of domain, where the only test that matters is deployment on unseen data.

\vspace{0.5cm}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} student engagement recognition, proposed hybrid model, feature-group temporal fusion, ordinal classification, cross-dataset generalisation.
