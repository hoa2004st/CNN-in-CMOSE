\begin{center}
    \Large{\textbf{ABSTRACT}}\\
\end{center}
\vspace{1cm}

Recognising how engaged a student is from webcam video can help teachers in online and hybrid classes, but it is difficult: the engagement labels are ordinal (Highly-Disengage, Disengage, Engage, Highly-Engage), heavily imbalanced, and partly subjective, and most studies do not have reliable evaluation for generalisation. Existing methods share two further weaknesses: they feed all of the facial features into one model as a single block, and they judge results mainly by accuracy, which stays high even when a model ignores the rare disengaged classes. In this thesis I address both problems. Because the facial features measure different behaviours that change at different speeds, I split the 709 OpenFace features into five groups (gaze, eye landmarks, face landmarks, head pose, and action units) and give each group its own temporal encoder, chosen from a Temporal Convolutional Network, an LSTM, or a Transformer; each group can also be combined with an I3D motion stream from the whole clip, and every stream has a small extra training head. Because accuracy is misleading on imbalanced ordinal labels, I select models with quadratic-weighted kappa (QWK) and report macro-accuracy and macro-MAE only as secondary checks. I make three contributions. First, I propose this feature-group hybrid and test all 243 ways of assigning encoders to groups, with and without I3D; on CMOSE the best hybrid reaches a QWK of 0.605 against 0.537 for the best single-encoder baseline, and only head pose clearly prefers one encoder, a TCN. Second, I collect and hand-label a private webcam test set, built with the same feature pipeline and kept only for testing. Third, I run a 3$\times$3 cross-dataset study that trains on CMOSE, DAiSEE, or both and tests on each public set and the private set. Models barely transfer: off-diagonal QWK falls to near zero even when accuracy still looks acceptable. On the unseen private set, training on both datasets with the hybrid gives the best result (QWK 0.379 against 0.285 for the best baseline). I conclude that quadratic-weighted kappa, not accuracy, must select engagement models when the labels are imbalanced.

\vspace{0.5cm}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} student engagement recognition, affective computing, OpenFace, I3D, temporal convolutional networks, ordinal classification, class imbalance, cross-dataset generalisation.
