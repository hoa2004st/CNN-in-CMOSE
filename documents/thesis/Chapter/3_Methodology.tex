This chapter specifies the methodology of the thesis. Section~\ref{sec:overview}
gives an overview of the complete pipeline as a single diagram.
Section~\ref{sec:data} describes the three training corpora, the self-collected
private test set, and the preprocessing that makes them comparable.
Section~\ref{sec:groups} formalises the semantic-group decomposition of the
OpenFace descriptor, and Section~\ref{sec:hybrid} builds the proposed
semantic-group hybrid architecture on top of it. Section~\ref{sec:baselines}
defines the monolithic baselines, Section~\ref{sec:losses-method} the exact loss
formulations, Section~\ref{sec:training} the training protocol, and
Section~\ref{sec:eval} the 3$\times$3 cross-dataset evaluation protocol and its
metrics.

\section{Overview}
\label{sec:overview}

The task is framed as four-class ordinal classification of a fixed-length clip.
The end-to-end pipeline is as follows. Per-frame OpenFace descriptors and a
clip-level I3D embedding are first extracted and brought to a common temporal
length, then normalised. The OpenFace descriptor is split into five semantic
groups; each group is encoded independently into a fixed 64-dimensional embedding
by its own temporal encoder, and, in the multimodal variant, the I3D clip vector
is encoded into a 128-dimensional embedding by a small MLP. The embeddings are
concatenated and classified by a shared multilayer perceptron, while an auxiliary
head supervises every stream separately. The monolithic baselines
(Figure~\ref{fig:baseline}) replace the split-and-encode stages with a single
encoder over the full 709-dimensional sequence; the proposed semantic-group hybrid
is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:hybrid}. Every model is trained and evaluated under a
3$\times$3 cross-dataset protocol and three loss functions.

\section{Data and Preprocessing}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Datasets}

The thesis uses three training sources and three test sets, whose sizes and class
balance are reported in Table~\ref{tab:T1_dataset_stats}.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{CMOSE}~\cite{cmose}: 12{,}197 clips, divided into a \texttt{train}
  split (8{,}783), an \texttt{unlabel} split (2{,}193) that this thesis uses as the
  validation set for early stopping and checkpoint selection, and a \texttt{test}
  split (1{,}221).
  \item \textbf{DAiSEE}~\cite{daisee}: 8{,}571 clips, whose official
  \texttt{Train}/\texttt{Validation}/\texttt{Test} partitions are mapped onto the
  same \texttt{train}/\texttt{unlabel}/\texttt{test} roles (5{,}358 / 1{,}429 /
  1{,}784).
  \item \textbf{Combined}: the union of CMOSE and DAiSEE (20{,}768 clips), with
  source-prefixed identifiers (\texttt{cmose\_\_}, \texttt{daisee\_\_}) so the two
  corpora cannot collide. All three splits are merged split-wise, giving 14{,}141
  / 3{,}622 / 3{,}005 train / validation / test clips.
  \item \textbf{Private (self-collected)}: a held-out set of \textbf{366 clips
  that we collected and manually labelled ourselves}, used \textbf{for testing
  only} --- never in training or model selection. Its construction is described in
  Section~\ref{sec:private-collection}.
\end{itemize}

All four sets share the four ordinal labels HD/DE/EG/HE. As
Table~\ref{tab:T1_dataset_stats} shows, the corpora are heavily imbalanced and,
crucially, imbalanced \emph{differently}: CMOSE is dominated by \emph{Engage}
(69.4\%) with only 2.8\% \emph{Highly\nobreakdash-Disengage}, whereas DAiSEE is
split between \emph{Engage} (50.3\%) and \emph{Highly\nobreakdash-Engage} (43.8\%)
with just 0.7\% \emph{Highly\nobreakdash-Disengage}. This mismatch in label priors
is central to the cross-dataset results of Chapter~4.

\subsection{The private evaluation set}
\label{sec:private-collection}

The private set is a deliberate attempt to measure deployment behaviour rather
than in-corpus behaviour, and it is the cleanest probe in the thesis because no
model is ever trained or selected on it. It was built in five steps.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Source and segmentation.} Webcam recordings of online-class
  sessions were sampled at several points within each session and cut into
  fixed-length \textbf{10-second clips}, each containing a single learner.
  \item \textbf{Tracking-quality filtering.} Every clip was run through the same
  OpenFace pipeline as the public corpora, and clips on which the face tracker
  succeeded on too few frames were rejected automatically (recorded as
  \texttt{too\_few\_success\_frames} in the acceptance manifest), since a
  descriptor built from unreliable frames would be meaningless.
  \item \textbf{Manual labelling.} The accepted clips were labelled by the author
  through a purpose-built local interface that plays each clip and records a
  single engagement label and optional notes. The label written to disk
  was always the author's own decision. Being produced by a \emph{single
  annotator} is a limitation that will be revisited in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.
  \item \textbf{Feature extraction.} Accepted clips were processed through the
  identical OpenFace (709-d per frame) and I3D (1024-d per clip) extraction used
  for CMOSE and DAiSEE, so the private features are distributionally comparable to
  the training features.
  \item \textbf{Final set.} Of the labelled clips, the \textbf{366} with complete,
  aligned OpenFace and I3D features form the private evaluation set. Its label
  prior differs again from both public corpora --- 58.2\% \emph{Engage}, 30.6\%
  \emph{Highly\nobreakdash-Engage}, 8.5\% \emph{Disengage}, and only 2.7\%
  \emph{Highly\nobreakdash-Disengage} --- which, together with its independent
  recording conditions, makes it the most realistic measure of how the models
  would behave in deployment. It anchors Section~\ref{sec:private}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Features and preprocessing}

\textbf{Features.} Each clip is represented by per-frame OpenFace~2.0 descriptors
(709-d)~\cite{openface2} and a precomputed I3D embedding (1024-d)~\cite{i3d}. When
a single OpenFace frame contains more than one detected face, the
highest-confidence row is kept; non-numeric entries are coerced to zero.

\textbf{Temporal resampling.} OpenFace clips vary in length, so each is resampled
along time to a fixed number of frames by linear interpolation per feature. The
OpenFace sequence length is \textbf{300 frames} for the single-corpus runs and is
reduced to \textbf{150} for the larger Combined runs to fit memory; in the
multimodal variant the OpenFace stream is resampled to \textbf{75} frames. The I3D
feature is a single 1024-dimensional vector per clip (not a sequence): the I3D
stream mean-pools it to that vector and encodes it with an MLP
(Section~\ref{sec:hybrid}).

\textbf{Normalisation.} Every feature is standardised by a per-feature
$z$-score whose mean and standard deviation are fit on the \textbf{training split
only}; the validation, test, and private sets are transformed with those training
statistics, so no target-set information leaks into preprocessing. OpenFace and
I3D are standardised with separate statistics.

\section{Semantic-Group Decomposition}
\label{sec:groups}

The 709 OpenFace features are partitioned into five behaviourally meaningful
groups by matching the column names, in the fixed order used everywhere in the
pipeline (gaze, eye, face, head, action units). Table~\ref{tab:groups}
summarises the partition; every one of the 709 columns belongs to exactly one
group.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{Semantic decomposition of the 709-dimensional OpenFace descriptor.}
\label{tab:groups}
\begin{tabular}{llr}
\toprule
Group & Content & Dim \\
\midrule
Gaze          & gaze direction vectors and angles            & 8   \\
Eye landmarks & 2-D/3-D eye-region landmark coordinates        & 280 \\
Face landmarks& 2-D/3-D facial landmark coordinates            & 340 \\
Head pose     & head translation and rotation parameters      & 46  \\
Action units  & facial action-unit presence and intensity     & 35  \\
\midrule
\textbf{Total} & & \textbf{709} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

This decomposition is the inductive prior of the method. The five subsystems have
different temporal dynamics --- rapid, noisy micro-saccades in the gaze and eye
groups; slow, smooth drift in head pose; brief, sparse bursts in action units ---
so each can in principle be matched to an encoder whose temporal bias suits it.
Encoding all 709 features together, as the baselines do, forces a single bias on
all of them at once.

\section{Hybrid Architecture (Proposed)}
\label{sec:hybrid}

The proposed model, shown in Figure~\ref{fig:hybrid}, encodes each stream
independently, fuses the resulting embeddings, and supervises every stream with
its own auxiliary head.

\textbf{Per-group encoder.} Each group is encoded by a \texttt{GroupEncoder} that
is one of three interchangeable temporal encoders, followed by temporal
mean-pooling and a layer normalisation, producing a fixed \textbf{64-dimensional}
embedding:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a \textbf{TCN} --- two residual blocks of dilated causal 1-D convolutions
  (channel widths $64,64$; kernel size 3; dropout 0.2);
  \item a \textbf{Transformer} encoder --- a linear projection to 64 dimensions, a
  sinusoidal positional encoding, and two encoder layers (4 heads, feed-forward
  width 128, GELU, dropout 0.2);
  \item an \textbf{LSTM} --- a two-layer recurrent encoder of hidden width 64
  (dropout 0.2).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Fusion and heads.} The five 64-d group embeddings are concatenated into a
320-d vector and passed through a two-layer MLP classifier
($320\!\to\!128\!\to\!4$, ReLU, dropout 0.3) that produces the main logits. Each
group additionally feeds an \textbf{auxiliary classification head}
($\mathrm{dropout}\to\mathrm{Linear}(64\!\to\!4)$). The total training loss is the
main-head loss plus $\texttt{aux\_weight}=0.2$ times the mean of the per-group
auxiliary losses (Section~\ref{sec:losses-method}); the auxiliary supervision
forces every stream to be individually discriminative.

\textbf{Multimodal variant.} The I3D-fused model adds a sixth stream: the single
1024-d I3D clip vector is encoded by an MLP ($1024\!\to\!256\!\to\!128$, ReLU,
dropout 0.3) and layer-normalised to a \textbf{128-d} embedding with its own
auxiliary head. The fusion MLP then takes a \textbf{448-d} input
($5\times64 + 128$). The I3D stream uses an MLP rather than a temporal encoder
because the provided I3D feature is a single pooled clip vector, not a sequence.

\textbf{Configuration and \texttt{arch\_key}.} A hybrid configuration is fully
specified by the encoder chosen for each of the five groups, written as an
\texttt{arch\_key} of five tokens in group order, for example
\texttt{TCN\_T\_TCN\_LSTM\_T} (\texttt{T}~=~Transformer, \texttt{TCN}~=~TCN,
\texttt{LSTM}~=~LSTM). With three choices per group there are
$3^{5}=\textbf{243}$ configurations, each evaluated with and without the I3D
stream (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\section{Baseline Models}
\label{sec:baselines}

Five monolithic baselines provide the comparison points; they form the allow-list
\texttt{MODEL\_ORDER} used by the analysis. Each consumes the full feature
sequence rather than the decomposed groups.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{openface\_mlp}: flattens the OpenFace sequence and classifies it
  with a multilayer perceptron ($\to$256$\to$128$\to$4, dropout 0.3).
  \item \texttt{openface\_tcn}: a TCN over the 709-d sequence (residual dilated
  blocks of widths $256,128,128$; kernel 3; dilations $1,2,4$), adaptive
  average-pooling, then a $128\!\to\!128\!\to\!4$ classifier.
  \item \texttt{openface\_lstm}: a two-layer LSTM (hidden width 256) whose final
  hidden state feeds a $256\!\to\!128\!\to\!4$ classifier.
  \item \texttt{openface\_transformer}: a linear projection to 128 dimensions, a
  sinusoidal positional encoding, and two Transformer encoder layers (4 heads,
  feed-forward width 256), mean-pooled and classified.
  \item \texttt{i3d\_mlp}: mean-pools the I3D clip feature to a 1024-d vector and
  classifies it with an MLP ($1024\!\to\!256\!\to\!128\!\to\!4$, dropout 0.3).
\end{itemize}
A sixth model, \texttt{openface\_tcn\_i3d\_fusion} (a shared-fusion TCN over both
modalities), exists in the codebase but is \textbf{excluded from the assessment}
so that the comparison isolates the semantic-group hypothesis rather than generic
modality fusion.

\section{Loss Functions}
\label{sec:losses-method}

Every model is trained under each of the three losses introduced in
Section~\ref{sec:losses-lit}: cross-entropy, weighted cross-entropy, and the
ordinal (EMD) loss of Equation~\eqref{eq:emd}. The class weights for the weighted
and ordinal variants are inverse-frequency weights computed on the
\textbf{training split} and normalised to mean~1: for class $c$ with $n_c$ training
samples and $C$ non-empty classes,
\begin{equation}
w_c = \frac{1}{n_c}\cdot\frac{C}{\sum_{k} 1/n_k},
\qquad\text{so that}\quad \frac{1}{C}\sum_c w_c = 1 .
\end{equation}
The ordinal loss is applied in its class-weighted form as well, multiplying each
sample's CDF distance by $w_y$.

For the hybrid models the objective also includes the auxiliary term. With main
logits $z$ and per-stream auxiliary logits $\{a^{(s)}\}$, the total loss is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{main}}(z,y) \;+\; 0.2\cdot\frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^{S}
              \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}\!\bigl(a^{(s)},y\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{main}}$ is the chosen loss (CE, weighted CE, or ordinal)
and the auxiliary heads are always trained with plain cross-entropy, over the
$S$ streams (five groups, or six with I3D).

\section{Training Protocol}
\label{sec:training}

All models are optimised with Adam~\cite{adam} at a learning rate of
$1\times10^{-4}$. Training uses mini-batches (128 for the baseline runs, 64 for the
243-configuration ablation) and \textbf{early stopping on the validation loss} ---
the CMOSE \texttt{unlabel} split, and its analogues for DAiSEE and Combined ---
with a patience of 10 epochs and an epoch cap (800 for baselines, 400 for the
ablation). The checkpoint with the lowest validation loss is restored for
testing. Following the convention adopted in this project, \textbf{only the
training and validation loss are recorded per epoch}; no per-epoch classification
metrics are computed, and the final classification metrics are evaluated exactly
once, on the held-out test split. Runs use a fixed random seed (42) and optional
automatic mixed precision on GPU. Representative loss curves
(Figure~\ref{fig:loss_curves_cmose_ce}, Chapter~4) confirm stable convergence and
early stopping well before the cap, so the differences reported between models are
architectural rather than optimisation artefacts.

\section{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:eval}

\textbf{Cross-dataset matrix.} Generalisation is measured with a 3$\times$3 matrix:
every model and loss is trained on each of \{CMOSE, DAiSEE, Combined\} and
evaluated on each of \{CMOSE-test, DAiSEE-test, Private\}. The diagonal cells are
the in-domain results; the off-diagonal and the Private column measure transfer to
unseen distributions.

\textbf{Metrics.} Each evaluation reports the six metrics defined in
Section~\ref{sec:metrics-lit} --- accuracy, macro-accuracy, MAE, macro-MAE,
Cohen's kappa, and QWK --- with \textbf{QWK and macro-accuracy as primary}.
Row-normalised confusion matrices and per-class F1 scores are used for error
analysis.

\textbf{Aggregation and reproducibility.} The complete study comprises roughly
1{,}500 trained runs: the five baselines under three losses across the three
training sources, and the 243 per-group hybrid configurations (each with and
without I3D) on CMOSE plus selected configurations on the other sources. All runs
are aggregated programmatically (\texttt{src/analysis/aggregate.py}), and the
figures and tables of Chapter~4 are produced from the aggregate by a single
command (\texttt{python -m src.analysis.make\_thesis\_artifacts}), so the entire
results chapter is reproducible from the trained runs.

\section{Chapter Summary}
\label{sec:method-summary}

This chapter specified the methodology end to end. It presented the pipeline and
the baseline architectures (Figure~\ref{fig:baseline}); described the three training corpora, the
test-only private set and how it was collected, and the leakage-free
preprocessing; formalised the five-group decomposition of the OpenFace descriptor;
and built the semantic-group hybrid architecture (Figure~\ref{fig:hybrid}),
including the per-group encoders, the fusion and auxiliary heads, the I3D variant,
and the 243-configuration design space. It then fixed the monolithic baselines,
the exact loss formulations with their class weights and auxiliary term, the
training protocol, and the 3$\times$3 evaluation protocol with its primary
metrics. Chapter~4 applies this methodology and reports the results.
