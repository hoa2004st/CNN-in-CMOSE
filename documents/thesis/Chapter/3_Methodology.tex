\FloatBarrier
\section{Overview}
\label{sec:overview}

The task is framed as four-class ordinal classification of a fixed-length clip.
Per-frame OpenFace descriptors and a clip-level I3D embedding are extracted, brought to a common temporal length, and normalised.
The monolithic baselines (Section~\ref{sec:baselines}) follow the standard recipe: a single encoder consumes the full 709-dimensional OpenFace sequence (or the pooled I3D vector) and feeds a classifier.
The proposed model replaces that single encoder with a split-and-encode design: the OpenFace descriptor is split into five semantic groups, each encoded independently into a 64-dimensional embedding by its own temporal encoder; in the multimodal variant the I3D clip vector is encoded into a 128-dimensional embedding by a small MLP.
The embeddings are concatenated and classified by a shared multilayer perceptron, while an auxiliary head supervises every stream separately; the hybrid is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:hybrid}.
Every model is trained and evaluated under a 3$\times$3 cross-dataset protocol and three loss functions.

\section{Data and Preprocessing}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Datasets}

The thesis uses three training sources and three test sets, whose sizes and class balance are reported in Table~\ref{tab:T1_dataset_stats}.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{CMOSE}~\cite{cmose}: 12{,}197 clips, divided into a \texttt{train} split (8{,}783), an \texttt{unlabel} split (2{,}193) that this thesis uses as the validation set for early stopping and checkpoint selection, and a \texttt{test} split (1{,}221).
  \item \textbf{DAiSEE}~\cite{daisee}: 8{,}571 clips, whose official \texttt{Train}/\texttt{Validation}/\texttt{Test} partitions are mapped onto the same \texttt{train}/\texttt{unlabel}/\texttt{test} roles (5{,}358 / 1{,}429 / 1{,}784).
  \item \textbf{Combined}: the union of CMOSE and DAiSEE (20{,}768 clips), with source-prefixed identifiers so the two corpora cannot collide.
  All three splits are merged split-wise, giving 14{,}141 / 3{,}622 / 3{,}005 train / validation / test clips.
  \item \textbf{Private (self-collected)}: a held-out set of \textbf{366 clips that we collected and manually labelled ourselves}, used \textbf{for testing only} --- never in training or model selection.
  Its construction is described in Section~\ref{sec:private-collection}.
\end{itemize}

All four sets share the four ordinal labels HD/DE/EG/HE.
Table~\ref{tab:T1_dataset_stats} and Figure~\ref{fig:dataset_class_distribution_overall} show that all three training corpora are heavily imbalanced toward the upper-middle classes, and --- crucially --- imbalanced in \emph{different directions}.
CMOSE is dominated by \emph{Engage} (69.4\%) with only 2.8\% \emph{Highly\nobreakdash-Disengage}, whereas DAiSEE is split between \emph{Engage} (50.3\%) and \emph{Highly\nobreakdash-Engage} (43.8\%) with just 0.7\% \emph{Highly\nobreakdash-Disengage}.
The rare disengaged classes are precisely the ones a useful monitoring system must catch, yet they amount to a fraction of a percent to a few percent of the data.
The split-wise distribution (Figure~\ref{fig:dataset_class_distribution_by_split}) confirms that this imbalance is consistent across the train, validation, and test partitions, so it is a property of the problem rather than of a particular split.

This severe and \emph{heterogeneous} imbalance shapes three choices the thesis returns to.
It predicts difficulty in transferring between corpora, where a model fit to one label prior meets a sharply different one at test time (Chapters~4 and~5); it motivates the imbalance-aware losses of Section~\ref{sec:losses-method}; and, because predicting the majority class alone scores well on every corpus, it rules out raw accuracy in favour of quadratic-weighted kappa, macro-accuracy, and macro-MAE as the \textbf{primary} metrics (Section~\ref{sec:eval}).

\subsection{The private evaluation set}
\label{sec:private-collection}

The private set measures deployment rather than in-corpus behaviour; because no model is ever trained or selected on it, it is the cleanest probe in the thesis.
It was built in five steps.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Source and segmentation.}
  Webcam recordings of online-class sessions were sampled at several points within each session and cut into fixed-length \textbf{10-second clips}, each containing a single learner.
  \item \textbf{Tracking-quality filtering.}
  Every clip was run through the same OpenFace pipeline as the public corpora; clips with too few successfully tracked frames were rejected automatically, since a descriptor built from unreliable frames is meaningless.
  \item \textbf{Manual labelling.}
  The accepted clips were labelled by the author through a purpose-built local interface, one engagement label per clip; reliance on a \emph{single annotator} is a limitation revisited in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.
  \item \textbf{Feature extraction.}
  Accepted clips were processed through the identical OpenFace (709-d per frame) and I3D (1024-d per clip) extraction used for CMOSE and DAiSEE, so the private features are distributionally comparable to the training features.
  \item \textbf{Final set.}
  Of the labelled clips, the \textbf{366} with complete, aligned OpenFace and I3D features form the private evaluation set.
  Its label prior differs again from both public corpora --- 58.2\% \emph{Engage}, 30.6\% \emph{Highly\nobreakdash-Engage}, 8.5\% \emph{Disengage}, and only 2.7\% \emph{Highly\nobreakdash-Disengage} --- which, together with its independent recording conditions, makes it the most realistic measure of how the models would behave in deployment.
  It anchors Section~\ref{sec:private}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Features and preprocessing}

\textbf{Features.}
Each clip is represented by per-frame OpenFace~2.0 descriptors (709-d)~\cite{openface2} and a precomputed I3D embedding (1024-d)~\cite{i3d}.
When a single OpenFace frame contains more than one detected face, the highest-confidence row is kept; non-numeric entries are coerced to zero.

\textbf{Temporal resampling.}
OpenFace clips vary in length, so each is resampled along time to a fixed number of frames by linear interpolation per feature.
The OpenFace sequence length is \textbf{300 frames} for the single-corpus runs and is reduced to \textbf{150} for the larger Combined runs to fit memory; in the multimodal variant the OpenFace stream is resampled to \textbf{75} frames.
The I3D feature is a single 1024-dimensional vector per clip (not a sequence): the I3D stream mean-pools it to that vector and encodes it with an MLP (Section~\ref{sec:hybrid}).

\textbf{Normalisation.}
Every feature is standardised by a per-feature $z$-score whose mean and standard deviation are fit on the \textbf{training split only}; the validation, test, and private sets are transformed with those training statistics, so no target-set information leaks into preprocessing.
OpenFace and I3D are standardised with separate statistics.

\section{Baseline Models}
\label{sec:baselines}

Five monolithic baselines provide the comparison points; they form the allow-list \texttt{MODEL\_ORDER} used by the analysis.
Each follows the standard recipe of the literature: a \emph{single} encoder consumes the full feature sequence and feeds a classifier.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{openface\_mlp}: flattens the OpenFace sequence and classifies it with a multilayer perceptron ($\to$256$\to$128$\to$4, dropout 0.3).
  \item \texttt{openface\_tcn}: a TCN over the 709-d sequence (residual dilated blocks of widths $256,128,128$; kernel 3; dilations $1,2,4$), adaptive average-pooling, then a $128\!\to\!128\!\to\!4$ classifier.
  \item \texttt{openface\_lstm}: a two-layer LSTM (hidden width 256) whose final hidden state feeds a $256\!\to\!128\!\to\!4$ classifier.
  \item \texttt{openface\_transformer}: a linear projection to 128 dimensions, a sinusoidal positional encoding, and two Transformer encoder layers (4 heads, feed-forward width 256), mean-pooled and classified.
  \item \texttt{i3d\_mlp}: mean-pools the I3D clip feature to a 1024-d vector and classifies it with an MLP ($1024\!\to\!256\!\to\!128\!\to\!4$, dropout 0.3).
\end{itemize}
A sixth model, \texttt{openface\_tcn\_i3d\_fusion} (a shared-fusion TCN over both modalities), exists in the codebase but is \textbf{excluded from the assessment} so that the comparison isolates the semantic-group hypothesis rather than generic modality fusion.

The five baseline architectures are shown in Figures~\ref{fig:openface_mlp}--\ref{fig:i3d_mlp}.
The four OpenFace baselines encode the full $T\times709$ sequence with a single encoder, while \texttt{i3d\_mlp} classifies the pooled 1024-d I3D clip vector.
Forcing one encoder --- and therefore one temporal bias --- onto all 709 heterogeneous features at once is exactly the design decision the proposed model revisits in Sections~\ref{sec:groups} and~\ref{sec:hybrid}.

\section{Semantic-Group Decomposition}
\label{sec:groups}

The 709 OpenFace features are partitioned into five behaviourally meaningful groups by matching the column names, in the fixed order used everywhere in the pipeline (gaze, eye, face, head, action units).
Table~\ref{tab:groups} summarises the partition; every one of the 709 columns belongs to exactly one group.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{Semantic decomposition of the 709-dimensional OpenFace descriptor.}
\label{tab:groups}
\begin{tabular}{llr}
\toprule
Group & Content & Dim \\
\midrule
Gaze          & gaze direction vectors and angles            & 8   \\
Eye landmarks & 2-D/3-D eye-region landmark coordinates        & 280 \\
Face landmarks& 2-D/3-D facial landmark coordinates            & 340 \\
Head pose     & head translation and rotation parameters      & 46  \\
Action units  & facial action-unit presence and intensity     & 35  \\
\midrule
\textbf{Total} & & \textbf{709} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

This decomposition is the inductive prior of the method.
The five subsystems have different temporal dynamics --- rapid, noisy micro-saccades in the gaze and eye groups; slow, smooth drift in head pose; brief, sparse bursts in action units --- so each can in principle be matched to an encoder whose temporal bias suits it.
Encoding all 709 features together, as the monolithic baselines of Section~\ref{sec:baselines} do, forces a single bias on all of them at once.

\section{Hybrid Architecture (Proposed)}
\label{sec:hybrid}

The proposed model, shown in Figure~\ref{fig:hybrid}, encodes each stream independently, fuses the resulting embeddings, and supervises every stream with its own auxiliary head.
It is built from the same three encoder families as the OpenFace baselines of Section~\ref{sec:baselines} --- TCN, Transformer, and LSTM --- but where a baseline applies one encoder to the full $T\times709$ sequence, the hybrid assigns an independently chosen encoder to each of the five semantic groups of Section~\ref{sec:groups}, so any difference in performance can be attributed to the decomposition rather than to new encoder machinery.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{hybrid.png}
\caption{Semantic-group hybrid architecture.
Each OpenFace group is encoded independently into a 64-d embedding (group encoders are chosen per group from TCN / Transformer (T) / LSTM); the I3D clip vector is encoded by an MLP into a 128-d embedding.
The six embeddings are concatenated (448-d) and classified by a shared MLP; each stream also feeds an auxiliary head.
The OpenFace-only variant drops the I3D row and fuses a 320-d vector.}
\label{fig:hybrid}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Per-group encoder.}
Each group is encoded by a \texttt{GroupEncoder} that is one of three interchangeable temporal encoders (Figure~\ref{fig:group_encoders}), followed by temporal mean-pooling and a layer normalisation, producing a fixed \textbf{64-dimensional} embedding:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a \textbf{TCN} --- two residual blocks of dilated causal 1-D convolutions (channel widths $64,64$; kernel size 3; dropout 0.2);
  \item a \textbf{Transformer} encoder --- a linear projection to 64 dimensions, a sinusoidal positional encoding, and two encoder layers (4 heads, feed-forward width 128, GELU, dropout 0.2);
  \item an \textbf{LSTM} --- a two-layer recurrent encoder of hidden width 64 (dropout 0.2).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Fusion and heads.}
The five 64-d group embeddings are concatenated into a 320-d vector and passed through a two-layer MLP classifier ($320\!\to\!128\!\to\!4$, ReLU, dropout 0.3) that produces the main logits.
Each group additionally feeds an \textbf{auxiliary classification head} ($\mathrm{dropout}\to\mathrm{Linear}(64\!\to\!4)$).
The total training loss is the main-head loss plus $\texttt{aux\_weight}=0.2$ times the mean of the per-group auxiliary losses (Section~\ref{sec:losses-method}); the auxiliary supervision forces every stream to be individually discriminative.

\textbf{Multimodal variant.}
The I3D-fused model adds a sixth stream: the single 1024-d I3D clip vector is encoded by an MLP ($1024\!\to\!256\!\to\!128$, ReLU, dropout 0.3) and layer-normalised to a \textbf{128-d} embedding with its own auxiliary head.
The fusion MLP then takes a \textbf{448-d} input ($5\times64 + 128$).
The I3D stream uses an MLP rather than a temporal encoder because the provided I3D feature is a single pooled clip vector, not a sequence.

\textbf{Configuration and \texttt{arch\_key}.}
A hybrid configuration is fully specified by the encoder chosen for each of the five groups, written as an \texttt{arch\_key} of five tokens in group order, for example \texttt{TCN\_T\_TCN\_LSTM\_T} (\texttt{T}~=~Transformer, \texttt{TCN}~=~TCN, \texttt{LSTM}~=~LSTM).
With three choices per group there are $3^{5}=\textbf{243}$ configurations, each evaluated with and without the I3D stream (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\section{Loss Functions}
\label{sec:losses-method}

Every model is trained under each of the three losses introduced in Section~\ref{sec:losses-lit}: cross-entropy, weighted cross-entropy, and the ordinal (EMD) loss of Equation~\eqref{eq:emd}.
The class weights for the weighted and ordinal variants are inverse-frequency weights computed on the \textbf{training split} and normalised to mean~1: for class $c$ with $n_c$ training samples and $C$ non-empty classes,
\begin{equation}
w_c = \frac{1}{n_c}\cdot\frac{C}{\sum_{k} 1/n_k},
\qquad\text{so that}\quad \frac{1}{C}\sum_c w_c = 1 .
\end{equation}
The ordinal loss is applied in its class-weighted form as well, multiplying each sample's CDF distance by $w_y$.

For the hybrid models the objective also includes the auxiliary term.
With main logits $z$ and per-stream auxiliary logits $\{a^{(s)}\}$, the total loss is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{main}}(z,y) \;+\; 0.2\cdot\frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^{S}
              \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}\!\bigl(a^{(s)},y\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{main}}$ is the chosen loss (CE, weighted CE, or ordinal) and the auxiliary heads are always trained with plain cross-entropy, over the $S$ streams (five groups, or six with I3D).

\section{Training Protocol}
\label{sec:training}

All models are optimised with Adam~\cite{adam} at a learning rate of $1\times10^{-4}$.
Training uses mini-batches (128 for the baseline runs, 64 for the 243-configuration ablation) and \textbf{early stopping on the validation loss} --- the CMOSE \texttt{unlabel} split, and its analogues for DAiSEE and Combined --- with a patience of 10 epochs and an epoch cap (800 for baselines, 400 for the ablation).
The checkpoint with the lowest validation loss is restored for testing.
Following the convention adopted in this project, \textbf{only the training and validation loss are recorded per epoch}; no per-epoch classification metrics are computed, and the final classification metrics are evaluated exactly once, on the held-out test split.
Runs use a fixed random seed (42) and optional automatic mixed precision on GPU.
Representative loss curves (Figure~\ref{fig:loss_curves_cmose_ce}, Chapter~4) confirm stable convergence and early stopping well before the cap, so the differences reported between models are architectural rather than optimisation artefacts.

\section{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:eval}

\textbf{Cross-dataset matrix.}
Generalisation is measured with a 3$\times$3 matrix: every model and loss is trained on each of \{CMOSE, DAiSEE, Combined\} and evaluated on each of \{CMOSE-test, DAiSEE-test, Private\}.
The diagonal cells are the in-domain results; the off-diagonal and the Private column measure transfer to unseen distributions.

\textbf{Metrics.}
Each evaluation reports the six metrics defined in Section~\ref{sec:metrics-lit} --- accuracy, macro-accuracy, MAE, macro-MAE, Cohen's kappa, and QWK --- with \textbf{QWK, macro-accuracy, and macro-MAE as primary}.
Row-normalised confusion matrices and per-class F1 scores are used for error analysis.

\textbf{Aggregation and reproducibility.}
The complete study comprises roughly 1{,}500 trained runs: the five baselines under three losses across the three training sources, and the 243 per-group hybrid configurations (each with and without I3D) on CMOSE plus selected configurations on the other sources.
All runs are aggregated programmatically, and the figures and tables of Chapters~4 and~5 are produced from the aggregate by a single command, so the entire results presentation is reproducible from the trained runs.

\section{Chapter Summary}
\label{sec:method-summary}

This chapter specified the methodology end to end: the pipeline; the three training corpora and their imbalance, the test-only private set and its collection, and the leakage-free preprocessing; the five monolithic baseline architectures (Figures~\ref{fig:openface_mlp}--\ref{fig:i3d_mlp}); the five-group decomposition of the OpenFace descriptor; and the semantic-group hybrid (Figure~\ref{fig:hybrid}) that reuses the baselines' encoder families per group, with its fusion and auxiliary heads, I3D variant, and 243-configuration design space.
It also fixed the loss formulations, the training protocol, and the 3$\times$3 evaluation protocol with its primary metrics.
Chapters~4 and~5 apply this methodology to the in-domain and generalisation results respectively.
