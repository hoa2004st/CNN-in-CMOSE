\section{Overview}
\label{sec:hybrid-overview}

Chapter~4 fixed the evaluation frame --- QWK, macro-accuracy, and macro-MAE as primary metrics; CMOSE as development corpus; cross-entropy as development loss; the OpenFace TCN at QWK 0.537 as the bar --- and supplied the design motivation: the baseline configurations, and the OpenFace and I3D feature families in particular, fail on different clips.
This chapter evaluates the proposed \textbf{semantic-group hybrid} (Section~\ref{sec:hybrid}) against that frame: all $3^{5}=243$ per-group encoder configurations, each with and without the I3D stream, trained with cross-entropy.
Section~\ref{sec:ablation} first re-validates the Chapter~4 frame on this 486-configuration population (RQ1 in part); Section~\ref{sec:group-marginal} isolates which behavioural group's encoder choice matters (RQ2); Section~\ref{sec:i3d-effect} measures the effect of I3D fusion with a paired ablation (RQ3); Section~\ref{sec:error} pins down the headline in-domain comparison (RQ1); and Section~\ref{sec:private} takes the hybrid out of domain, establishing that it generalizes better than the baselines --- in \emph{every} cell of the matrix, and most of all on the decisive self-collected private set, where the architecture and the corpus-pooling lever \emph{compound} (RQ4).
Section~\ref{sec:gen-discussion} discusses why.
As in Chapter~4, family-level claims are reported as means and distributions and headline comparisons as the tuned maximum (Section~\ref{sec:baseline-overview}).

\section{Consistency with the Baseline Frame}
\label{sec:ablation}

The ablation population doubles as a stress test of Chapter~4's protocol: 486 configurations of a different architecture family, none of which existed when the frame was fixed.
Figure~\ref{fig:hybrid_ablation_all_metrics} plots the distribution of the two hybrid families (with and without I3D) on every metric, each panel against the best base model on that metric.
Three findings of Chapter~4 reproduce.

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.72\linewidth]{hybrid_ablation_all_metrics.png}
\caption{Hybrid ablation across all 243 per-group encoder configurations, with and
without I3D, on all six metrics (in-domain CMOSE). Each dashed line is the best base
model on that metric, across all three losses.}
\label{fig:hybrid_ablation_all_metrics}
\end{figure}

\textbf{The metric structure reproduces.}
Recomputing the Kendall rank correlation between the six metrics over the 486 in-domain configurations yields the same two-block picture as Section~\ref{sec:protocol-metrics}: a micro block ($\tau=0.87$), a macro block ($\tau=0.73$), weak cross-block agreement, and QWK again the only bridge ($\tau=0.63$--$0.73$ with both blocks) --- a property of the metrics on this task, not of any model family.

\textbf{Accuracy is still blind.}
In Figure~\ref{fig:hybrid_ablation_all_metrics} every configuration sits in a narrow accuracy band around the best baseline's 0.773 (top-left panel), so a model chosen on accuracy would see no architecture signal at all, while the QWK panel separates the families cleanly.
(The macro panels' dashed lines belong to the \emph{ordinal-loss} I3D MLP, a balanced-loss operating point that buys those figures by giving up QWK --- the loss trade-off of Section~\ref{sec:loss-tradeoff}, not an architecture effect.)

\textbf{The cross-domain collapse reproduces.}
Taken out of domain (Section~\ref{sec:hybrid-crossdomain}), the hybrid matrix shows the same shape as the baseline matrix: a healthy diagonal (0.605 on CMOSE, 0.228 on DAiSEE) and an off-diagonal collapse, with accuracy again masking it.
The collapse is a property of the \emph{problem} --- divergent label priors and recording conditions --- not of a particular architecture.

All three pillars of the Chapter~4 frame thus survive a 32-fold larger model population; the protocol carries over without amendment (RQ1's frame condition).

\section{Encoder Choice per Group}
\label{sec:group-marginal}

The 243-configuration grid varies five group$\rightarrow$encoder choices at once; this section marginalises them to find which group's choice actually moves the primary metrics (RQ2), and whether any preference is corpus-specific or survives shift.

Table~\ref{tab:T12_group_marginal_combined} reports the marginal in both regimes.
\textbf{In-domain}, only \textbf{head pose} shows a clear preference: TCN (mean QWK 0.545), ahead of LSTM (0.541) and Transformer (0.523), a spread of 0.022; macro-MAE agrees, lowest (best) under the TCN.
The other four groups are essentially encoder-agnostic (QWK spread $\le0.006$).
\textbf{Under shift} (the same marginal over the unseen-target cells), head pose again has the largest spread (0.010) and again prefers the TCN, while the other groups' spreads (0.002--0.004) flip winners arbitrarily --- noise.
\input{Table/T12_group_marginal_combined}

The consistency across regimes elevates the head-pose rule from a tuning accident to a property of the signal: head dynamics such as nodding and turning away are temporally local and convolution-friendly (RQ2).
The design space is otherwise flat, so most of the architecture's benefit must come from \emph{decomposing and fusing} rather than from the per-group encoder search --- which is what the next section measures.

\section{Effect of I3D Fusion}
\label{sec:i3d-effect}

Chapter~4 predicted, from the error-overlap analysis of Section~\ref{sec:openface-vs-i3d}, that fusing the I3D stream should pay.
This section measures the prediction with the strongest design in the grid: \textbf{pairing each OpenFace-only configuration with its exact +I3D twin} (same five encoders), so the distribution of paired QWK differences \emph{is} the I3D effect, free of the configuration as a confounder, in three evaluation regimes.

\textbf{Where the test corpus is seen in training, fusion pays decisively.}
Over the seen-target cells the mean paired gain is \textbf{+0.060 QWK, and 94\% of the 972 pairs improve}.
In-domain on CMOSE this is the cash value of the complementarity of Section~\ref{sec:openface-vs-i3d}: the I3D-fused family is centred above the OpenFace-only family (median QWK 0.553 against 0.522, visible in Figure~\ref{fig:hybrid_ablation_all_metrics}), and \textbf{82\% of the 243 fused configurations clear the 0.537 baseline bar}, against 26\% of the OpenFace-only ones.
The gain is a property of the \emph{family}, not of one lucky configuration (RQ3).

\textbf{Beyond the training corpora, the gain evaporates.}
Cross-corpus the mean paired effect is \textbf{$-0.021$ QWK with only 26\% of pairs improving}; on the private set it is +0.005 with 52\% improving and very high variance ($\pm0.2$).
I3D's global appearance features drag corpus bias along --- the same mechanism behind the I3D MLP's fall from second to fourth in the architecture ranking under shift (Section~\ref{sec:crossdomain-matrix}).
The practical rule (RQ3): \emph{fuse I3D when the target domain is represented in training; rely on the OpenFace groups otherwise} --- a rule about the family average (the single best private-set model does carry I3D, but it is one tail of a wide, zero-centred distribution).

\section{Comparison with Baselines}
\label{sec:error}

\textbf{Best hybrid versus best baseline.}
Table~\ref{tab:T4_hybrid_topk} lists the top-10 configurations; the best overall is the I3D-fused hybrid \texttt{TCN\_T\_TCN\_LSTM\_T}, and every top configuration mixes at least two encoder types.
On all three primary metrics the best hybrid improves on the best baseline --- \textbf{+0.068 QWK} (0.605 versus 0.537), macro-accuracy 0.582 versus 0.535, and macro-MAE 0.489 versus 0.547 (lower is better).
All three move in the same direction, so the gain is real --- but \textbf{modest}; it is the CMOSE diagonal cell of the cell-by-cell comparison in Section~\ref{sec:hybrid-crossdomain} (Figure~\ref{fig:crossdomain_delta}).
\input{Table/T4_hybrid_topk}

\textbf{Where does the gain land?}
The per-class F1 scores (Figure~\ref{fig:per_class_f1}) localise it.
Relative to the best baseline, the hybrid improves recall on the ordinal extreme \textbf{Highly\nobreakdash-Engage} ($0.38\to0.51$) and on \textbf{Disengage} ($0.45\to0.55$), while keeping \emph{Engage} near 0.90.
The rarest class \textbf{Highly\nobreakdash-Disengage}, however, stays unsolved and is slightly traded away (recall $0.40\to0.37$): the model reallocates capacity toward the more frequent extreme.

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.62\linewidth]{per_class_f1.png}
\caption{Per-class F1: best base versus best hybrid (in-domain CMOSE).}
\label{fig:per_class_f1}
\end{figure}

The in-domain improvement is therefore real but \textbf{uneven}: the hybrid clears the baseline on all three primary metrics by strengthening the well-populated upper-engagement region and the middle disengaged class, while the $\sim$2--3\%-frequency \emph{Highly\nobreakdash-Disengage} class remains the principal open challenge (RQ1).
The next section shows the picture becomes more severe still out of domain --- and that the architecture's advantage over the baseline \emph{widens} there.

\section{Cross-Dataset Generalization}
\label{sec:private}

\subsection{Cross-Dataset Matrix}
\label{sec:hybrid-crossdomain}

The hybrid cross-dataset heatmap (Figure~\ref{fig:crossdomain_hybrid}) shows the same pattern as the baseline matrix of Section~\ref{sec:crossdomain}: a healthy diagonal and an off-diagonal collapse of all three primary metrics --- the hybrid does not rescue cross-corpus transfer.
\textbf{The accuracy deception persists}: on DAiSEE$\rightarrow$CMOSE the best QWK any of the 486 hybrid runs achieves is 0.10, yet hybrids in that cell post accuracies up to 0.69.
A second architecture family thus lands in the same place --- the metric frame is not an artefact of the monolithic models --- and this is the last time accuracy appears in the thesis's analysis.

\textbf{Within the collapse, the hybrid wins every cell.}
Figure~\ref{fig:crossdomain_delta} subtracts the baseline matrix (Figure~\ref{fig:crossdomain_base}) from the hybrid matrix (Figure~\ref{fig:crossdomain_hybrid}) cell by cell: the best hybrid beats the best baseline in \textbf{all nine train$\times$test cells}, with every cell of the $\Delta$QWK matrix positive.
The gains are smallest on the in-domain diagonal ($+0.068$ on CMOSE) and \textbf{largest on the decisive Private column} ($+0.118$, $+0.050$, $+0.094$ from the three training sources).
The improvement is not a single tuned point but a uniform shift of the entire matrix --- the strongest form the family-level claim can take --- and, read column by column, the figure makes the chapter's two headline comparisons one object: its CMOSE diagonal cell is the in-domain gain of Section~\ref{sec:error}, and its Private column is the private-set result developed next.

\subsection{The Private Set}
\label{sec:private-results}

The most consequential evaluation in this thesis is on the \textbf{private set} --- the 366 clips we collected and hand-labelled ourselves (Section~\ref{sec:private-collection}), processed through the identical OpenFace/I3D pipeline and used \textbf{for testing only}.
Because no model is ever trained on it, it is a genuine ``in-the-wild'' probe of deployment, with its own recording conditions and label prior (58\% Engage, 31\% Highly-Engage, only 2.7\% Highly-Disengage); the \emph{only} design lever is the choice of training source.
The Private column of Figure~\ref{fig:crossdomain_delta} and Table~\ref{tab:T6_private_by_source} report the best base model and the best hybrid per training source, and two effects compound.
\input{Table/T6_private_by_source}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Training source matters, and the combined corpus wins.}
  For both model families, training on \textbf{Combined} generalises best to the private set (best base QWK: Combined 0.285 versus DAiSEE 0.256 versus CMOSE 0.190) --- pooling heterogeneous source corpora is the most effective simple route to an unseen target, exactly as Chapter~4 predicted.
  \item \textbf{The hybrid generalizes better --- and helps more here than in-domain.}
  The hybrid beats the best base model on QWK for \emph{every} training source, and the gap is largest exactly where it matters: under combined training, \textbf{QWK 0.379 versus 0.285}, a \textbf{$+0.094$} improvement --- wider than the $+0.068$ in-domain gain, so distribution shift \emph{amplifies} rather than erodes the architecture's advantage.
  The overall best private-set model is the combined-trained I3D-fused hybrid \texttt{T\_T\_T\_LSTM\_T}, best on both ordinal primaries: \textbf{QWK 0.379} and the lowest \textbf{macro-MAE 0.877} (against the best base's 0.997).
  (One caveat in Table~\ref{tab:T6_private_by_source}: the CMOSE-only base model posts a deceptively low macro-MAE (0.75) despite the weakest QWK by predicting conservatively around the majority classes --- the false comfort that reporting all three primaries together is designed to catch.)
\end{enumerate}

Figure~\ref{fig:private_confusion_combined} compares the confusion of the two combined-trained models on the private set.
The hybrid captures the ordinal structure markedly better: \emph{Engage} recall rises from 0.46 to 0.72 and errors concentrate on the diagonal and its neighbours, where the base model scatters \emph{Engage} mass into \emph{Highly-Engage} (0.44).
As in-domain, the rare disengaged classes are the failure mode --- hybrid \emph{Disengage} recall is only 0.29, and \textbf{Highly\nobreakdash-Disengage collapses to 0.00} (all ten private HD clips are misread).

\subsection{In-Domain versus Transfer}
\label{sec:hybrid-indomain-transfer}

Section~\ref{sec:indomain-vs-transfer} showed that a baseline's in-domain QWK barely predicts its transfer ($\rho=0.28$); the ablation population sharpens the warning.
Figure~\ref{fig:indomain_vs_generalization_hybrid} overlays the 972 trained hybrid models on the baseline scatter: within the hybrid family the correlation is \textbf{negative} ($\rho=-0.31$) --- among closely related configurations, squeezing out more in-domain QWK mildly \emph{hurts} transfer, because the extra fit is corpus fit.
The hybrid's advantage is therefore not a position on an in-domain--transfer trend --- no usable trend exists --- it is a \textbf{lift of the whole trade-off}: the hybrid cloud sits above the baselines at every level of in-domain performance, and the private-set winner is found by family-level robustness and pooled training, not by picking the best in-domain configuration.

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.56\linewidth]{indomain_vs_generalization_hybrid.png}
\caption{In-domain QWK versus mean unseen-target QWK with the hybrid ablation population
overlaid on the baseline configurations. Within the hybrid family the
correlation is negative ($\rho=-0.31$): more in-domain fit means more corpus fit. The
hybrid cloud sits above the baselines throughout --- the architecture lifts the
trade-off rather than riding it.}
\label{fig:indomain_vs_generalization_hybrid}
\end{figure}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:gen-discussion}

\textbf{Why the levers work.}
Pooling works through prior coverage: CMOSE and DAiSEE are imbalanced in opposite directions (Section~\ref{sec:data}), so their union spans a wider range of label priors and an unseen target sits closer to the pooled corpus than to either single source.
The hybrid's margin \emph{widens} off-domain for the reason it was designed: encoding each behavioural channel separately, each forced to be discriminative through its auxiliary head, yields a representation less tied to any one corpus's idiosyncrasies than a monolithic encoder free to latch onto a corpus-specific shortcut --- the negative in-domain--transfer correlation ($\rho=-0.31$) is the same mechanism from the other side.
The two feature families diverge under shift because the OpenFace descriptors live in subject-centred physical units that mean the same thing in any setup, whereas the I3D embedding encodes global appearance --- exactly what changes between corpora --- so fusion adds signal only where the test appearance statistics are represented in training.

\textbf{What the headline number means.}
QWK 0.379 is a clear improvement over chance, every single-source model, and the best baseline ($+0.094$), but in absolute terms \emph{moderate}: with macro-MAE 0.877 the model captures the broad engaged-versus-disengaged trend yet is unreliable at fine distinctions, and the rarest class \emph{Highly\nobreakdash-Disengage} fails in both regimes.
It is usable as an aggregate, classroom-level signal but not to flag an individual disengaged learner without human confirmation; the open problem is one of data and objective --- no encoder assignment can recover a class the training distribution barely contains --- which is why the imbalance-specific remedies of Chapter~6 are the natural next step.

\section{Chapter Summary}
\label{sec:hybrid-summary}

The Chapter~4 frame survived the 486-configuration stress test: the metric block structure, the blindness of accuracy, and the cross-domain collapse all reproduced as task properties.
Only head pose has a clear encoder preference (a TCN), so the benefit comes from decomposing and fusing rather than the encoder search (RQ2); I3D fusion is decisively positive where the test corpus is seen in training, neutral-to-harmful beyond it (RQ3).
In-domain the best hybrid beats the best baseline on all three primary metrics (QWK 0.605 versus 0.537, RQ1); out of domain it wins \emph{every} cell of the matrix, compounding with combined-corpus training to the best private-set model (QWK 0.379, $+0.094$ over the baseline --- wider than in-domain), and lifts the whole in-domain--transfer trade-off rather than riding it (RQ4), with Highly\nobreakdash-Disengage the principal failure mode in both regimes.
Chapter~6 draws the findings together, revisits the research questions, and outlines future work.
